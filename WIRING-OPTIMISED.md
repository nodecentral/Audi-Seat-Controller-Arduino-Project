# Optimised design (prototype, not built or tested)

Text version of [`images/enclosure-wiring-optimised.png`](images/enclosure-wiring-optimised.png), generated by [`tools/enclosure_wiring_diagram_optimised.py`](tools/enclosure_wiring_diagram_optimised.py).

This is the current build ([`WIRING.md`](WIRING.md), [`images/enclosure-wiring.png`](images/enclosure-wiring.png)) with three additions:
- **D1:** a diode that makes the wake button easier to press successfully
- **C1:** a capacitor that stretches a short button press
- **OLED:** a 0.96" display

Everything not listed under "What changes" is identical to `WIRING.md`. That includes:
- the J1 and J2 terminal blocks and their pin assignments, so the same harness plugs in
- the whole 12V and motor side
- the YYLOCK-2 terminal positions
- fuses F1/F2 and pull-downs R1/R2
- tests T1–T4

**Build from `WIRING.md` until this design has been bench-tested.**

## Why each change

### D1: 1N5819 Schottky diode in the self-latch wire (W18)
**The problem:** the self-latch wire joins YYLOCK `NO` to `DC+` permanently. So while the button is held, before the relay has closed, the button feeds more than the module's `DC+`. Through the self-latch wire it also feeds everything on `NO`:
- the Cytron's supply, with its capacitors
- the buck converter
- the Nano behind the buck converter

The button has to carry all of that until the relay pulls in. That is the likely reason a light or short press doesn't always wake the system. This is reasoning from the circuit, not a measurement. The bench check: meter `DC+` to `DC-` during a light press, and look for a dip well below 12V.

**The fix:** D1 lets current flow only from `NO` into `DC+`.
- **When pressed:** the button now only has to power the YYLOCK module itself.
- **Once the relay closes:** `NO` holds `DC+` up through D1, about 0.3V lower, so roughly 11.7V on a 12V feed. That is fine; a car runs at 12.6–14.4V.
- **Orientation:** the band (cathode) goes towards `DC+`. Fitted backwards, the latch will not hold after the button is released.

### C1: 330µF 25V electrolytic across DC+ / DC-
C1 holds `DC+` up for a moment if the button contact is short or bouncy. With D1 fitted it only has to feed the module, so a small capacitor is enough.
- **Fitting:** solder it to the underside of the blue DC block's pins, so no extra conductors go into the screw terminals.
- **Polarity:** + leg to `DC+`, striped (−) side to `DC-`.
- **Rating:** 105°C rated preferred for a car interior.
- **At timeout:** C1 does not change the timeout behaviour. When the relay opens, its charge only drains away.

### OLED: 0.96" 128×64 I2C display (SSD1306 type, 3–5V)
- **Pins:** the Nano's I2C is fixed to `A4` (SDA) and `A5` (SCL).
- **Recline moves:** recline therefore moves from `A5` to `A0`. It can't go to `A6`/`A7`, because they don't support `INPUT_PULLUP`, which the switch panel needs. `A1`/`A2` stay reserved for the parked tilt axes.
- **Mounting:** in a window in the enclosure lid. The diagram shows it from the back, as seen from inside the box, so its pins read `SDA, SCL, VCC, GND` left to right, the reverse of the front silkscreen.
- **Cable:** keep the I2C wires short, under about 30cm.
- **Soldering:** the module has bare pads. Solder the wires on for vibration resistance.

### WAGO B: second WAGO 221-415, the logic ground hub
The Nano's two GND terminals already hold two wires each, so the OLED's ground needs somewhere new to go. WAGO B gathers the low-current logic grounds and links them to the Nano's analog-row GND with a single wire. The Nano's two GND pins are joined on the Nano's own board, and the digital-row GND still links to WAGO A (W30), so both hubs are one ground.

## Firmware changes needed (not written yet)
- **Recline pin:** in `seat_control_main.ino`, change recline's pin from `A5` to `A0`.
- **Display code:**
  - Set up the motor pins first in `setup()`, then the display, so a slow or missing display can never delay the motors being held off.
  - Keep display updates out of the motor timing, so the 8s run cutoff stays accurate.
  - Use a light library (SSD1306Ascii, or U8g2 in page mode). The Nano has 2KB of RAM, and a full-frame buffer would take half of it.
- **What the display could show:** time left before the YYLOCK-2 switches off, counted from the Nano's power-up, since the timer starts then and a re-press doesn't extend it. Also which axis is moving, the cutoff tripping, and raw switch readings for measuring PINs 1/2.

## Extra checks before power-up (in addition to WIRING.md T1–T4)
1. **D1:** on the meter's diode test from `NO` (red probe) to `DC+` (black probe), expect about 0.2–0.4V. Reversed, it should read open.
2. **C1:** + leg on `DC+`, before any power is applied.
3. **Wake test:** a short, light press now wakes the system every time. Repeat T3 in full, because D1 and C1 sit on the latch circuit.
4. **OLED:** with the new firmware, the display shows text and the motors still respond normally. If the display is unplugged, the motors must still be held off and still work.
